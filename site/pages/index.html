For private-sale vehicles in British Columbia

Pay PST on what your vehicle is really worth.

When you register a privately bought vehicle, ICBC collects PST on the higher of your purchase price or the Canadian Black Book average wholesale value. If high kilometres, damage or mechanical problems make your vehicle worth less, our appraisal can lower the value you're taxed on.

Is it worth it?

An appraisal usually helps when your vehicle has

High kilometres

Well above average for its age. Black Book's average wholesale value assumes average kilometres.

Mechanical problems

Engine, transmission, warning lights, or repairs a mechanic has quoted.

Body damage or rust

Dents, scrapes, cracked glass, rust on rockers or wheel wells.

Accident history

Previous claims, a rebuilt status, or frame repairs that lower resale value.

Worn interior

Tears, stains, smells, broken trim or controls that don't work.

Tires or brakes due

Upcoming maintenance a buyer would subtract from the price.

If you paid Black Book value or more, an appraisal won't lower your PST. Save your money.

Common questions

Quick answers

Is this allowed?

Yes. BC's PST rules allow a vehicle appraisal from a registered dealer or a professional appraiser. If the appraised value and your purchase price are both below the Black Book average wholesale value, PST is charged on the higher of those two.

Do I need to bring the vehicle anywhere?

No. Everything is done from your details and photos. We may ask for more photos if we need them.

I already registered. Is it too late?

Usually not. You can apply for a refund within 30 days of registering. See After registering.
